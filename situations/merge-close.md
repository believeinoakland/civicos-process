# Situation: merging a job; closing a layer, a round, the tranche (P7, P10, P12, P14)

- **Merge** as soon as a job is COMPLETE, every COMPLETE job in one background run: `proc merge --modules ready --context <your context %, from get_session>` (or `--modules <m1,m2>`; upstream first, in `modules.json` order; each gated as below; a refused one is reported and the others go on). One job: `proc merge --module <m> --context <%>` (the figure goes in the ruling, K5167). It refuses unless the record reads COMPLETE; runs ownership against the merge base, merges with a merge commit, regenerates and verifies every generated artifact the manifest lists, runs the module's tests and its scoped checks (about 16 s on bio) and undoes the merge if anything is newly red (old reds and findings it inherited are named, never a reason to refuse), strikes the met `*(not yet met: T<n>)*` marks the report names, writes the ruling line, pushes and verifies on origin. For an extraction, read every `ADDED` line ownership lists. `proc merge` merges nothing red: a red is fixed by the job, or its entry re-scoped with the job (`situations/messages.md`, blocked). Two jobs each red without the other (a new fleet member and the test listing the fleet) merge together: `proc merge --module <m> --with <m2>` (one commit, each gated as if alone).
- **Close a layer** when every job of it is merged and none is re-opened: `proc layer close --layer <L>` (it regenerates and verifies the artifacts again and runs every check over the whole product once, refusing only findings new since the tranche opened); archive each session it lists (`archive_session`), writing each row with `proc job archived --module <m> --usage '<the usage object archive_session returns, as JSON>'`; start the next layer.
- **Close a round** after its last layer: `proc round close` (archives its plan whole); open the next round at once if it has entries.
- **Close the tranche** after its last round: merge the design stream's PR if it asked (`MERGE`), then `main` into the tranche (never a rebase; first `proc history --ref origin/main`, which fetches the history that merge needs), run every module's tests and the checks, set the plan's Status `CLOSING`, open the PR `tranche/T<n>` to `main` and merge it with the GitHub merge tool (method `merge`; the hook allows it only once CLOSING); `proc prune --apply` (the merged job and prep branches, deleted by the product's `prune-branches` workflow, which checks each again; never the restore point, a study, or an open tranche's); delete your backstop; report to Bob in plain words (never ask for or raise his weekly meter, K5136): elapsed time, what was finished, what was left and why, what it cost (P14, P19), and its footprint in the repository from `proc footprint` (commits by kind; the share that is bookkeeping). Open the next tranche at once.
