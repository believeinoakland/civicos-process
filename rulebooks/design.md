# DES · Design · a question that needs Bob's decision, and how a lane's decision becomes canon, a rule or a requirement

In force from 2026-10-29, certified on N1. At most ten rules, one screen, each checkable. Tags: YOURS (Bob's policy) or BOB'S; CODE names what enforces it, TEXT means nothing does.

1. Bob decides only policy and doctrine, the high-level architecture and UX, and a requirement only where it changes what the product does for its users and no canon section answers it; every other decision, an edge case of a module or an area included, is BOB's or its lane's, made without asking him, recorded once and reported as done. (P17, P5; Bob K2055, K5036; Bob, 29 Oct: "you should not be stopping to involve me in these details") YOURS · TEXT.
2. BOB routes each request in this order and writes the route with its reason: answer it (BOB's by mechanics §7), ask the lane that owns the area, or bring it to Bob. A question a ruling already answers is not asked again. (P17, P15; K1437, K2009) BOB'S · CODE: `proc ask route`; the already-answered part is TEXT.
3. A question to a lane is put in the lane's terms: the canon section or rule at issue, the behaviour needed, the options, what the answer must settle; never the requester's code or branch. A lane that needs another area asks BOB, never the lane. (P20; PROTOCOL §1, §5) BOB'S · CODE: the lane's hook refuses a `send_message` to any address but BOB's; TEXT for the terms.
4. A question for Bob carries an explanation he can understand, the options and a recommendation, rendered from the thing he will use; the ask reads `NEEDS BOB` until he answers, and nothing else waits on it. A doubtful case is decided by BOB, which says which way it read it. (P17, P20; K2055) YOURS · CODE: `proc status` lists the ask `NEEDS BOB`; TEXT for the content.
5. A lane answers with a `DECISION`: the decision in one or two sentences, its rationale, its evidence, what it changes (a canon section, a rule, or what a requirement means) and whether any of it is Bob's. (P20, P5; PROTOCOL §5) BOB'S · CODE: `proc ask decided` refuses an answer that does not begin `DECISION`; the parts are TEXT.
6. A decision has one home: a canon design is written by the lane on the tranche branch; a rule is drafted by the lane and rewritten in place by BOB; a requirement is written by BOB, citing the canon section. (P5, P15; DESIGN-STAGE §1) BOB'S · TEXT.
7. The change is committed in its home with the ask's close, before the requester is answered, and the reply gives the requirement or entry as it now reads. A wording-only change to a requirement is BOB's and waits for no one. (P15, P5; DESIGN-STAGE §2, mechanics §7) BOB'S · CODE: `proc ask close` refuses without the changed files in the same commit.
8. A change to what a requirement means waits for Bob's decision; then BOB adds an entry for every module that uses the changed service: a CHANGE to its running job, else its START, else the completion round. (P5, P10, P19; DESIGN-STAGE §2) BOB'S · CODE: `proc ask close` refuses a `needs Bob` ask without his decision cited; the entries are TEXT.
9. Each decision is one line in the lane's `decisions.md`: `L-<ID>-<n> · date · ask · decision · where folded`. A rule or requirement cites that id, never a ruling number or an ask file; Bob's direct direction to a lane is a line marked `Bob`. (P15; DESIGN-STAGE §3, §4) BOB'S · TEXT.
10. A principle, or a rule tagged YOURS, changes only after Bob has seen the exact before-and-after and approved it in words; his answer to another question is not approval. A text that changes no principle is BOB's. (P16, P17; K1258, K5036) YOURS · TEXT.
