# GOV · Governance · who decides what; how a rule is made, changed and retired

In force from 2026-10-29, certified on N1. At most ten rules, one screen, each checkable. Tags: YOURS (Bob's policy) or BOB'S; CODE names what enforces it, TEXT means nothing does.

1. Bob decides only policy and doctrine, the high-level architecture (layers; modules that carry product capability), UX, and a requirement by DES 1's test. Every lower-level matter, a module's or an area's edge case included, is BOB's: decided without asking, recorded once, reported to Bob as done. (P17; K2055, K1257; Bob, 29 Oct) YOURS · TEXT.
2. Nothing stops for Bob: a session told to keep going keeps going until there is no way to; a question reaches him only by DES 1's test, through the record, with an explanation, options and a recommendation, and never as a turn that ends waiting on him; a doubtful case is BOB's, decided and reported with which way it read it. BOB never tells Bob it will do something without doing it in the same turn; a turn that must wait ends with one line naming what it waits on and, unless it is Bob's, `Nothing for Bob to do.` (P17, P19; Bob, 29 Oct: the process overcomes the habit of stopping to involve him, strongly and consistently) YOURS · CODE: `proc ask route --bob` refuses without `--users` and `--canon-silent`; BOB's and a lane's Stop hook refuses a turn that ends on words waiting for Bob.
3. A principle changes only after Bob has seen the exact change, rendered, and approved it in words; an answer to another question is not approval. (P16; K1258) YOURS · TEXT.
4. Any other process text (mechanics, tools, rulebooks) is BOB's to change. Every rule names the principle it serves, and none is added in reaction to an incident unless a principle yields it. (P16; K5036, K113) YOURS · TEXT.
5. A process change is certified before product work uses it: checked against the principles, then proven on a dry-run tranche, each path in the certification list proven or named as provable only in a product run, with the check that will prove it. A meaningful flaw stops product work until then. (P3; K111) BOB'S · TEXT.
6. A lane's decision on a rule is drafted by the lane; BOB rewrites the rulebook in place, and Bob sees before and after if the rule is his. It is written in its home before the requester is answered. (P15; DESIGN-STAGE §1, §2) BOB'S · CODE: `proc ask close` refuses a reply whose files are not in the same commit.
7. A decision is recorded once: a lane's as an `L-<ID>-<n>` line, BOB's or Bob's as one line `K<n> · date · area · ruling · why`, each changed only by a new line naming its evidence and the line it replaces, never reopened without new evidence named in writing. A rule cites only the latest decision that sets it. Every act a ruling owes is an entry in `next.md` or the running plan in the same commit as the ruling; a ruling never holds owed work alone. (P15, P19; DESIGN-STAGE §3; K3676) BOB'S · CODE: `proc ask close` and `proc ruling` number them; the format check refuses an id given twice.
8. A ruling of Bob's quotes his words and adds no limit he did not state; BOB's additions are marked "BOB's details (P17)"; where they differ, his words govern. (P15; K1762) YOURS · TEXT.
9. A session acts on what it created: it archives only its own children, and a role's successor is started by that role's parent. Bob's standing approval of every development and testing act is on record (PERMISSIONS §6), so no session asks him for one; an act only he can take is named to him in one line, walked through, and nothing waits on it; BOB never approves an act for Bob. (P13, P17; PROTOCOL §7a; PERMISSIONS §2.3, §6) YOURS · CODE: the act table's routes; `proc status` lists `RELAY TO BOB`.
10. Merges and every other repository act are BOB's and never asked of Bob; the one development act he takes is approving a GitHub release. (P17; K5346) YOURS · TEXT.
