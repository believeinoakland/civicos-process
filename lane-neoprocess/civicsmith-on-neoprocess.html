1What Civicsmith supplies

The process asks a product for its canon, its build state, its areas and its own procedures (neoProcess §7). Civicsmith's build state is already in build/ of its repository, with its manifest naming where everything lives. Its ten product areas follow its doctrine, not its build order. Each owns its canon documents and its modules; a module's job gets its area's rules by path. The audit

An independent Opus reviewer checked 20 merged T53 jobs against their requirements without seeing the jobs' conversations. It found 19 defects, under every model. The model followed the kind of work (Haiku had word conversions, Sonnet behaviour changes, Opus whole new modules), so the per-model rates compare different work, and three Opus jobs is too few for a rate.

The most serious finding is a privacy defect in mail-door. A message sent again after a member brought it in has its private bytes stored again, and they are never deleted (index.mjs:375, :384, confirmed by the reviewer with a test). The rule it breaks: private bytes are held only until bring-in or for 14 days.

Other findings. money had three tests named for R39–R41 with empty bodies, which passed and counted as coverage (its open job has since replaced them). civil-time marks R31 met, but its "spans two fiscal years" answer does not name the government asked about. register-readers runs out of memory on large registers keyed by content, against its R1. Ten of the 94 rulings since K5700 correct an earlier BOB act; BOB merged three jobs whose reports claimed work the merge does not contain.

What it shows. The gate checks that a test names each requirement, not that it proves it. The Integration rulebook's rules 2 and 4 (neoProcess §5) close that gap. The audit cost about 36,000 tokens per job and did all 20 in 28 minutes. Each defect becomes an entry for its module in T53's completion round, mail-door's first; placing them changes no code, so it does not cross your stop.

3The order of work

  1. You decide what is yours on the neoProcess page (its §8), and the stop on product work stays until step 6.
  2. Codify. For each area, a worker reads that area's sources whole and drafts its rulebook, accounting for every ruling in its slice: kept as a rule, folded into a requirement or design, made code, superseded by a named rule, or retired with its reason. BOB reviews every line; you read only the rules that are yours, area by area, rendered.
  3. Trim the requirements to what each module does now, so every job can read its own whole (P6). History and citation chains go to the archive.
  4. Rebuild the tooling to the rulebooks: a pull request per job; the gate and the reviewer in CI; the merge queue; start skills that load each role's areas; path-scoped product rules; size limits checked in CI. PROCESS-REPAIR's merge-queue, bundles-out-of-git and R2 work carries straight in.
  5. Clean the repository in place. Screenshots, bundles and finished records leave the working tree; history stays untouched. The repository keeps its address, because every team's copy checks believeinoakland/bio's main for update notices and the installer fetches each release from it; a brand-new repository would cut every team off. BOB #333 estimates a 150 MB checkout afterwards, against 723 MB today.
  6. Prove it with a dry run (P3), then resume T53: layer 5's last two jobs, the audit's defects, then layer 6.

BOB #333's estimate is about a week of elapsed time, not measured. The merge queue also answers most of the waiting your slowdown analysis measured: finished jobs queued for BOB to merge by hand.

Decisions here

A · The product areas

  • Recommended: the ten above.
  • The same, with changes you name.
  • Fewer and broader; each rulebook longer or coarser.

B · The order of work

  • Recommended: build the new process and clean the repository in place together, prove it, then resume T53.
  • A brand-new repository under the same name, the current one renamed to an archive. A clean history, but every version tag the installer fetches must be carried across, and this environment cannot push tags, so that part needs you on GitHub.
  • Resume T53 now on the old process while the new one is built. Faster product work, on the process you have stopped, with the audit's defects still getting through.

By P17 the order of work is BOB's except for the stop on product work, which is yours; it is put to you because lifting the stop decides it.
