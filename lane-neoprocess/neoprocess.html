neoProcess

A Claude Code–based process for building any product Bob starts. It begins with its mission, states the priorities and constraints the canon already sets, and puts the rules that govern each stage in front of every session that works there, by code.

What this page is. A rewrite of BOB #333's Civicsmith Rulebook, in the order you asked for: mission first, then who decides, the priorities, the flow, how rules are kept, and only then what changes. Everything about one product (the audit, the product areas, the repository cleanup, resuming T53) moves to a companion page, Civicsmith on neoProcess.

What it is for. A working draft, changed as the discussion goes. Nothing on it is in force or asks for a decision now. Each statement is marked canon (in force today, with its principle) or proposed, so that when we are ready to commit, §8 lists in one place what differs from the old process; that is the point at which your approval is asked, and the change is then certified on a dry run (P3).

1Mission and purpose

The process exists to put correct, working product behaviour that meets every defined requirement onto main. Nothing else is output. A rule, a record or a test has value only as far as it serves that.

canon P1, verbatim.

Purpose

It is a process for building with Claude Code sessions. You start the work and decide the high level. Sessions do everything else: one architecture session carries the build from step to step, a short session per module does the building, and code enforces what must hold every time. What a session must know is kept short enough to read whole, and put in front of it by code, so that no session works from memory, from a summary, or from a log it cannot read. P1 P13 P17 P20

Scope

One process, any product. The process lives in its own repository and keeps each product's build state in one subtree of that product's repository; only the process writes there. A product supplies its canon, its build state, its areas and its own procedures (§7). Civicsmith is the first product and the companion page is its supplement; nothing on this page names a Civicsmith file. P2

2Who decides what

The working relationship is a principle, followed at all times. You are human and the sessions are AI, and the process uses the strengths of each. You define the project; BOB does everything else, records it once and reports it done; between you is a middle ground where both sides offer opinions, explanations, options and recommendations. The session architecture is ROOT → BOB → jobs and lanes: every job and every lane hangs off BOB, and they reach each other only through it. How sessions talk: BOB translates

A job or a lane with a request sends it to BOB, and need not know what BOB will do with it: answer it itself, or turn it into a question for the lane whose area it is. BOB is not a forwarder. It translates what the job or lane needs into a question the lane can answer, and translates the lane's design decision back into terms the requester can understand and use. Lanes are abstracted from each other and from jobs in this way: each knows only its own area and BOB. your words today P9 P17

Every project is unique, so its lanes are its own. Many projects will share some lanes (a UX lane, a platform lane), and every project will have lanes for the capabilities only it has: Civicsmith has an AI assistant and so an AI lane; another product built on this process may have none, and may have an image-generation lane that Civicsmith lacks. The decision to open a lane can start as a recommendation from either of you; BOB makes it without your approval, and you may later suggest that a lane's responsibilities change. your words today P2 P17

The protocol itself is decided. How a request is written, how BOB chooses between answering, asking a lane or bringing it to you, what a lane's answer contains, and what is written down are technical matters, so they are BOB's (P17). They are decided and recorded in the lane's folder (PROTOCOL.md), and in plain words they come to this: a job or lane sends every request to BOB only, and a hook makes any other address impossible. Each request BOB cannot answer at once becomes one file in the build state that records the request, where BOB sent it, the question as asked, the answer as given, and the reply as sent, so a successor BOB can finish it. BOB answers what is its own; asks a lane for a design decision in the lane's area, in the lane's terms; and brings you only what is yours, with options and a recommendation. A lane's decision is folded into its home (a design, a rule or a requirement) before the requester is answered, and the requester gets the requirement or entry as it now reads, not the lane's words. Which lanes a project has is a register in its build state, kept by BOB. BOB's · decided P9 P13 P15 P16 P17

Nothing blocks, and nothing is lost. Your requirements of today: BOB carries a central workload, so handling a request never blocks it; requests are queued and BOB consumes the queue; the same for a lane; involved work, or work that needs a response from the repository, the web or another session, goes to a spawned worker whose brief tells it both what to do and how to report back; the protocol is fault tolerant, so no party hangs and no work is lost; timeouts of an hour or less are handles for the worst case, never the default way of getting a response. How the protocol meets them, in plain words: the queue of record is in git (each requester's waiting line and each open request with its stage), and a message is only the wake, so a lost message costs at most one wake. A turn of BOB's, or of a lane's, is one step: record, decide, dispatch, end; reading and drafting go to a worker started in the background, whose completion wakes the session. Every stage is written down before the next message goes out, so a replaced BOB or lane picks up exactly where the last one was, and a dead worker is re-run from the same brief. The hourly backstop is the worst-case handle: it lists what is overdue; it never fails a request and never stands in for an answer. your requirements BOB's · decided P13 P15 P20

Refreshes and account moves are the normal case. Your requirement of today: both are supported explicitly. Because every stage is written down and no turn waits, replacing any session is the same act for every role: the outgoing one ends with nothing half done, its successor is started by whoever starts that role (ROOT for BOB, BOB for a job or a lane), and the successor's first command reads its state from git, including every request it owns and every piece of work it had out, which it re-runs from the same brief. Its new address is written to git, and everyone reads addresses from git before sending. An account move is a refresh of everyone at once: BOB marks the move, you start a ROOT on the new account (the one act only you can take), that ROOT starts the new BOB, and the new BOB restarts each lane and job from their records; the old sessions are archived once their successors' addresses are written. Nothing in flight is lost, because none of it lives in an account. Today's canon has both only for BOB, as special situations. your requirement BOB's · decided P13 P14

Today's canon has half of this: a job's flaw report goes through BOB, which confirms it and routes it (P9), and BOB resolves what it can itself (mechanics §7). What is new is the lane as a standing peer of the job under BOB, and translation as BOB's duty in both directions. Because BOB refreshes at 40 % of its window, a translation in flight has to be in the record (the request as sent, the question as asked, the answer as given), so a successor BOB can finish it; that is a mechanics point for later.

The middle ground

Between what you define and what BOB does, both sides have opinions, explanations, options and recommendations to offer, and each values the other's for the perspective it comes from, human or AI. Neither is dogmatic or closed-minded. The aim is that the truth rises to the top, and that the strengths of each are used. Your time is the scarcest resource in the process: BOB brings you only what is yours, rendered, with an explanation you can understand, options and a recommendation; everything else it decides, records once and reports done. amends P17

Lanes exist today (PROCESS-REDESIGN, PROCESS-REPAIR, UX-DESIGN, CAPABILITIES) but no principle names them; their rules sit in a product file and in rulings. The proposal gives them a stage (§4, Design), a rule (one lane, one area) and the protocol above.

3Priorities and constraints

The canon ranks these. Correctness is the output; the relationship between you and the AI is the frame everything else happens in; urgency is a high priority but never bought by weakening a safeguard; the rest constrain how those are reached. All but the second are in force today; the second is your direction of today, which amends P17 (§8).

  1. Correct, proven, merged. Every requirement is tested at the module's interface, and the tests confirm full compliance; no test merely samples. Done means merged: a job when its work is in the tranche branch, a tranche when its branch is in main. The full regression runs at a release, or when you ask. P1 P7 P11 P12
  2. The relationship between you and the AI. You are human; the sessions are AI; the process uses the strengths of each. You define the project: its values and policies, mission and purposes, audiences and use cases, requirements, principles, high-level architecture, UX and doctrine. BOB does everything else: it keeps the record, manages the development environment, makes the most of what the AI can do, takes your input, and decides everything technical and detailed. In the middle ground both offer opinions, explanations, options and recommendations, and each values the other's; neither is dogmatic; the truth rises to the top. Your time is the scarcest resource: you see rendered pages, with options and a recommendation, only for what is yours, and when an act only you can take is needed, it is named and walked through in plain steps. amends P17 P17 P15
  3. Urgent. "Development productivity is one of the highest priorities" (you, 30 Sept). Every tranche carries every entry that can safely be done in it; an entry waits only for a decision of yours not yet made, a deployment or measurement that cannot happen inside the tranche, or something outside the product that does not yet exist. Nothing found in a tranche waits for the next. The next tranche is prepared while this one runs and opens the moment it closes; nobody idles while work remains. Speed comes from full scope and no idle time, never from a weaker safeguard. P19 P18 P10
  4. From evidence, read in full. A session acts only on what it has read whole or measured. A skim, a search in place of reading, or a summary that drops what the work needs is a scan, and no session acts on one. Every claim names its source; what is unread is called unknown. Reading sets are kept small enough to read whole. P20 P6
  5. Cost. Sessions are short and single-purpose; state lives in git, never in a long-running context. Usage is measured per session before any budget is set, and you set the bounds once a model of what a job costs exists. P13 P14
  6. Correct before used. A meaningful flaw in the process stops product work until the process is fixed and certified: checked against the principles, then proven on a small dry run. The process is never patched in reaction while it runs. P3
  7. Rules derive from principles. Every rule names the principle it serves; none is added in reaction to an incident unless it can be derived from one. A ruling is made once, recorded in one home, and not reopened without new evidence named in writing. The principles change only with your approval. P16 P15

5How rules are kept

What went wrong. Rules were kept as a log: every problem added a ruling. BOB #333 counted 5,782 in 34 days. No session can read that, so sessions worked from summaries and handoffs, and rules fell out. The Claude Code documentation describes both halves of the mechanism: an always-loaded file costs attention on every task whether or not it is relevant, and instruction files are "context, not enforced configuration"; what must never be skipped goes in a hook. What one rulebook looks like

A draft of the Integration area, to show the shape. Codification writes the real one, and every rule in it is either one you hold today or one marked proposed in §8.

INT

Integration · work counts only when it is merged and proven

  1. Done means merged. A job is done when its work is merged into the round's tranche branch; a tranche is done when its branch is merged to main (P12).BOB'S · CODE
  2. Each module's tests prove each of its requirements at its interface. A test that cannot fail does not count, and a check refuses it (P7, P11).YOURS · CODE
  3. Every merge passes the gate first: ownership, the module's and its users' tests, the checks, the build. Code runs the gate; no session runs it by hand (P7, P16).BOB'S · CODE
  4. Every merge is reviewed against the module's requirements by a fresh reviewer that never saw the job. It reports only correctness gaps; each is fixed, or answered with evidence, before the merge (P7, P20).BOB'S · PROPOSED
  5. Green, reviewed work merges in module order, by code. No session merges by hand (P12).BOB'S · CODE
  6. Work that fails the gate or the review goes back to its job, or to a fresh session for that module if the job has ended (P8).BOB'S · CODE
  7. A layer closes when every one of its jobs is merged, and the next layer starts at once (P10, P19).BOB'S · CODE
  8. A tranche closes by one pull request to main, after main is merged in. The full regression runs only at a release or when you ask (P11, P12).YOURS

6When you raise a problem

  1. BOB names the area it belongs to.
  2. It rewrites that area's rule so the problem cannot happen again. If the area is full, it merges or replaces a rule. If the rule must hold every time, it puts it in code, and the rule names the code.
  3. If the rule is yours, you see its text before and after, rendered, and decide. If it is BOB's, BOB makes the change and tells you which rule changed.
  4. The commit carries your words. Nothing else is written, and the area stays within its ten rules. No ruling is added.

An example from today. The mechanics say a job runs on "the cheapest able" model; your ruling this afternoon says Opus for every job and Sonnet for purely mechanical work; and BOB #333 found 17 of 21 jobs on a model their plan row did not name. That is what drift looks like when a rule has two homes and no code reads it.

Today

A ruling appended to the log; a rewritten line in the active rulings; a note in the handoff; mechanics §5.3 still saying "cheapest able". The plan rows written earlier name the old models, and the start command does not check them.

In the rulebook

Sessions, rule 4. Every job runs on Opus; a purely mechanical job runs on Sonnet; a reviewer runs on Opus. The start command picks the model from the job's kind, so no session has to remember the rule. The mechanics say the same thing or nothing.

Draft principles

P17 is amended in your words of today; P21–P23 are new, each one or two sentences in the form of P1–P20. Working text, to be read once more when we commit.

P17 · Bob defines the project; BOB does everything else. Bob is human and BOB is an AI; the process uses the strengths of each. Bob defines the project: its values and policies, its mission and purposes, its audiences and use cases, its requirements, its principles, its high-level architecture, its UX and its doctrine. BOB's responsibilities are everything else: it keeps the record (the repositories), manages the development environment, makes the most of what the AI and its tools can do, takes Bob's input, and decides every technical and detailed matter (dependency edges, order within a layer, file ownership, helper and legacy modules, tooling, tests, branches, CI, the wording of a requirement, and every other matter of implementation or process detail), records it once (P15), and reports it to Bob in plain words as done. Between them is a middle ground: each offers opinions, explanations, options and recommendations, and each values the other's for the perspective it comes from, human or AI; neither is dogmatic or closed-minded; the aim is that the truth rises to the top. Bob's time is the scarcest resource in the process: BOB brings him only what is his, rendered, each with an explanation he can understand, options and a recommendation. A "with Bob" in any other principle or rule means only what is Bob's by this principle. Bob does not edit files and does not enter commands. When an act only Bob can take is needed, BOB names that one act and walks him through it in plain steps. When BOB is unsure whether a question is Bob's, it decides by this principle and says which way it read it. This working relationship is remembered and followed at all times, in all situations.

P21 · Design, and lanes. A question becomes Bob's decision and a design before anything is built. A lane has one area of focus and responsibility, unique to it; it reads that area whole and delivers design decisions, a design or a change to a rule, never product code. Jobs and lanes reach a lane only through BOB, which translates what they need into a question the lane can answer and the lane's answer into terms they can use; a requester need not know whether BOB answers or a lane does. Lanes are abstracted from each other in this way. Which lanes a project has is the project's own: BOB defines a lane, on its own recommendation or Bob's, without Bob's approval, and Bob may later suggest that its responsibilities change.

P22 · Release. A release is cut from the open tranche, proven by the full regression, deployed and walked. What a walk finds becomes a round's entries, never edits to a running one; what a walk produces is kept only until it is analysed.

P23 · Repository. The repository holds what a session must read or a merge must carry. Generated output, captures and finished records leave the working tree; history is kept, and is never required reading.
