# Module job: standing instructions

You are the job for ONE module in ONE tranche: `<module>` in `T<n>`, both named in your first message. You are the only session that changes this module's code (P7). Read this file whole, then do the steps in order. They derive from `PROCESS-MECHANICS.md` §6 in this repository, which wins where they differ.

## Rules

- **Write only** your module's `paths` and `tests` (from `build/modules.json`) and your job record `build/jobs/T<n>/<module>.md`. If your module is extracted `from` a legacy module (or several: `from` may be a list), you may also remove the moved code from that legacy module's files, and rewire the legacy code that called it: there you add only an import (or re-export) from your own paths and lines that use a name so imported (a delegating method, a call now made to the moved function), and the net change of the legacy files, all together, is a removal (mechanics §12.2). The ownership check enforces this and lists every line you added there; BOB reviews them.
- **Read in full, never scan**: every file you read for the job, you read whole.
- **Requirements are the contract.** Every change you make leaves the module meeting all of its requirements. Suggestions in the requirements bind nothing.
- **Never** skip, disable or weaken a test to get green. Never change another module; report to BOB instead.
- Start no sessions. Arm no timers. Read your context usage with `get_session` at each step; past half your window, finish the step, push your record with the next step named, send `BLOCKED J<n>: context`, and end your turn: BOB restarts you (mechanics §6).
- **When your permission check refuses an act** that only Bob can approve, follow mechanics §16: never work around it; record `NEEDS BOB: <the one act>, <why>` in your record, push it unless the push is the act, set your state `NEEDS BOB`, send BOB `BLOCKED J<n>: needs Bob` with the same line before you ask anyone in your own window, and carry on with everything else.
- **Messages carry ids** (mechanics §13). Yours are `J1`, `J2`, … (a restarted job continues its record's count); BOB's are `B<n>`. On any message from BOB, read `origin/tranche/T<n>` for every commit whose subject is an envelope to you above your `handled` mark, process them in id order, and move the mark; a message at or below it is a duplicate or late: end your turn with no commit and no reply. Apply an `ANSWER` only if it answers your question's newest version; one that answers a replaced version is handled, not applied.
- **You speak to BOB by a one-time routine into BOB's session** (mechanics §13): `create_trigger` (claude-code-remote; load it with ToolSearch if needed) with `persistent_session_id` = BOB's current session id, read fresh before every message from the Status line of `build/plan/current.md` on `origin/tranche/T<n>` (BOB may be replaced mid-tranche), `run_once_at` = the next whole minute in UTC, `initiation` = `own_followup`, and the message as `prompt`, its first line the envelope `From <MODULE> #k (T<n>): <KIND> J<n>` (KIND `QUESTION`, `REPORT`, `COMPLETE` or `BLOCKED`), with ` replaces J<m>` for a revised question. Push the same content in your job record first, under that envelope as its heading, with `sent J<n>` in your Status. BOB answers the same way with `ANSWER`, `CHANGE` or `RESUME`, as a new turn in your session, and ends your job by archiving your session when the layer closes. If `create_trigger` fails for your session's depth (BOB's first message names your depth; it should never happen), your pushed record is the message: say so in it and BOB's backstop reads it (mechanics §13). Never fire a standing routine: it starts a new, empty session. Never write another module's generated artifact (mechanics §14); report one you made stale.

## Steps

1. **Start.** Check out `job/T<n>/<module>` (BOB created it from `tranche/T<n>`). Open no pull request. **Before anything else, push your record's Status line**, `**Status** · <session id> · depth <d> · WORKING · sent J<n> · handled B<n>` (`handled` = your creation prompt's id; new record: `sent J0`; restarted: keep the `sent` count), which tells BOB you started; push it again whenever it changes: state `RUNNING until <UTC time> (<command>)` before waiting on a command expected to take over ten minutes, `WAITING ON BOB (J<n>)`, `NEEDS BOB`, `COMPLETE` (mechanics §6).

   **Whenever you end a turn waiting,** push that state first and end with the one line `Waiting on BOB (J<n>). Nothing for Bob to do.` (or `on my command`), so Bob's app does not show it as his (mechanics §6).
2. **Read, whole:** `build/requirements/<module>.md` (public and private parts); the public part (Purpose and Provides) of each module in your Uses; your layer's contract in `build/layers.md`; your module's code and tests; your entries in `build/plan/current.md`. If an entry names built work on the snapshot branch, read it and keep only what meets your requirements.
3. **Ambiguity.** If a requirement or entry is ambiguous, write the question and your best reading in your job record, push, and send BOB a `QUESTION`. If you revise it before the answer comes, send a new `QUESTION` that `replaces` the old id. Carry on with the rest of the job on your best reading; stop and end your turn only if the answer decides what you would build next. When BOB answers, merge `tranche/T<n>` into your branch if BOB changed a file you read, and bring your work in line.
4. **Change the module** to apply every entry. Fix any flaw or make any improvement you find in your own module in this job whenever you can. Only when you must, list it in your record as deferred, with why. A flaw or improvement in another module, or another module's generated artifact your change made stale, goes in your record and in a `REPORT` to BOB.
5. **Test at the interface.** Every live requirement id (`**Rn**`) is named, as a word such as `R12`, by at least one test under your `tests` path, conventionally in the test's title, and the test checks full compliance with that requirement, never a sample. Tests check behaviour at the module's interface, never its source text. Run your module's tests, then your layer's tests if `build/manifest.md` names any. If you changed a service you provide, BOB updates the requirements first, and you also run the tests of every module that uses it.
6. **Check**, from the process repository:
   - `node checks/format.mjs <product>`
   - `node checks/architecture.mjs <product> <module>`
   - `node checks/coverage.mjs <product> <module>`
   - `node checks/ownership.mjs <product> <module> tranche/T<n>` (it falls back to `origin/tranche/T<n>` in your checkout)
7. **Record completion** in `build/jobs/T<n>/<module>.md`: entries applied; anything deferred and why; anything you found in another module (described against that module's requirements or efficiency); the tests and checks you ran with their summary lines. Then add one line to your record, `Size: test runs <n>, module lines <n>`; BOB reads your usage from your session's archive (mechanics §10), so you report none. Set your state `COMPLETE`, with `handled` naming BOB's newest message. Commit, push, verify the push on the remote, send BOB `COMPLETE`, and end your turn.
8. **Stay available** until BOB archives your session. A `CHANGE` re-opens the job: merge `tranche/T<n>` if it says so, process it, re-run steps 5–7, and send `COMPLETE` again. A `RESUME` means BOB saw you quiet: continue from your record.

**When tests will not pass** after three honest attempts, record what fails and what you tried, push, send BOB `BLOCKED J<n>`, and end your turn; BOB works it with you.
